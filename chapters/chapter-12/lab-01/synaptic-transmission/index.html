Chapter 12 · Lab 1 · Part 1

Synaptic transmission & initiating the impulse

How a chemical message released at a synapse becomes an electrical signal and, if it reaches threshold at the axon hillock, an action potential.

In development. This page outlines the planned simulation. The interactive version will reuse the lab's shared playback controls and membrane model. Part 2 is fully interactive now.
Learning outcomes

After this part, students will be able to…

  • Describe how an arriving action potential opens voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels in the axon terminal and triggers exocytosis of neurotransmitter.
  • Explain how neurotransmitter binding opens ligand-gated (chemically gated) channels in the postsynaptic membrane.
  • Distinguish excitatory (EPSP) from inhibitory (IPSP) postsynaptic potentials, and graded potentials from action potentials.
  • Explain temporal and spatial summation and why threshold must be reached at the axon hillock (trigger zone).
  • Describe how the signal is ended (enzymatic breakdown, reuptake, diffusion).
How it connects

Hand-off to Part 2 and Part 3

When summed postsynaptic potentials bring the axon hillock to threshold (≈ −55 mV), an action potential begins. That action potential is the signal that enters from the left edge of the axon in Part 2 (unmyelinated) and Part 3 (myelinated).

Planned clinical connections. Myasthenia gravis (antibodies against acetylcholine receptors), botulinum toxin (blocks neurotransmitter release), acetylcholinesterase inhibitors, and SSRIs (block serotonin reuptake).
Planned simulation storyboard

What the animation will show, step by step

  1. Action potential arrives at the presynaptic axon terminal (the same waveform used in Part 2).
  2. Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels open; Ca²⁺ flows in down its steep gradient (shown with its own particle colour).
  3. Vesicles dock and fuse (exocytosis); neurotransmitter is released into the synaptic cleft.
  4. Neurotransmitter diffuses across the cleft and binds receptors on the postsynaptic membrane.
  5. Ligand-gated channels open: mainly Na⁺ entry gives an EPSP; Cl⁻ entry or K⁺ exit gives an IPSP. The student chooses an excitatory or inhibitory synapse.
  6. Graded potential spreads and fades with distance toward the axon hillock (decremental conduction), shown on a voltage-vs-distance plot.
  7. Summation: students fire one or several synapses, close together in time (temporal) or from different locations (spatial), and watch whether the trigger zone reaches threshold.
  8. Threshold reached → action potential at the axon hillock, with a button to follow it into Part 2 or Part 3.
  9. Signal ends: neurotransmitter is broken down, taken back up, or diffuses away; receptor channels close.

Planned controls

Shared playback bar (play, step, next stage, speed); synapse type (excitatory / inhibitory); number and timing of presynaptic spikes; a toggle to block reuptake or the enzyme; a probe at the axon hillock with a live voltage-vs-time trace and threshold line.
