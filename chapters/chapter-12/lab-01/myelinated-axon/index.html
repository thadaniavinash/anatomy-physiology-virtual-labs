Saltatory conduction in a myelinated axon

Myelin insulates the axon between the nodes of Ranvier. Voltage-gated Na⁺ and K⁺ channels are found only at the nodes, so the action potential is regenerated only there and appears to “jump” from node to node.

Why myelin matters

Why saltatory conduction is faster

1 · Fewer places to regenerate the APOpening channels and regenerating the action potential is the slow step. In an unmyelinated axon it happens at every patch of membrane; in a myelinated axon it happens only at the nodes.
2 · Myelin insulatesCurrent cannot leak out through the myelin, so the positive charge that enters at one node spreads quickly and strongly to the next node.
3 · Less work, less energyIons cross the membrane only at the nodes, so fewer ions move and the Na⁺/K⁺ pump uses less ATP to restore them.

Real speeds: thin unmyelinated fibres about 0.5–2 m/s; large myelinated fibres up to about 120 m/s.

Key points
  1. Myelin is a fatty insulating wrap made by oligodendrocytes (CNS) and Schwann cells (PNS). The membrane under it has almost no ion channels.
  2. Nodes of Ranvier are short gaps between myelin segments, packed with voltage-gated Na⁺ and K⁺ channels.
  3. At each node the action potential goes through the same phases as in Part 2: 1 depolarization, 2 repolarization, 3 hyperpolarization, 4 return to resting potential.
  4. Saltatory conduction: Na⁺ entering at one node spreads under the myelin to the next node, which reaches threshold and fires. The AP appears to jump from node to node. Refractory nodes behind it keep the signal moving forward.

Simplifications: nodes are drawn about 1 mm apart and much wider than real nodes (about 1 µm) so the channels can be seen. Speeds and timing are slowed for teaching.

Clinical connection. In multiple sclerosis the immune system damages myelin in the brain and spinal cord; in Guillain–Barré syndrome it damages myelin in peripheral nerves. Current then leaks out before reaching the next node, so conduction slows or fails, causing weakness, numbness and other neurological symptoms.
