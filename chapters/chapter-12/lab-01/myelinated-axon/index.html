Saltatory conduction in a myelinated axon

Myelin insulates the axon between nodes of Ranvier. Current flows quickly under the myelin, and the action potential is regenerated only at the nodes — so the signal appears to "jump" from node to node.

In development. This page outlines the planned simulation. It will reuse the membrane model from Part 2 at each node, so channel states and ion flow match exactly between the two axon types.
Learning outcomes

After this part, students will be able to…

  • Identify myelin (Schwann cells in the PNS, oligodendrocytes in the CNS), internodes and nodes of Ranvier.
  • Explain why voltage-gated Na⁺ channels are concentrated at the nodes and why the action potential is regenerated only there.
  • Describe how myelin reduces current leakage and membrane capacitance, so local current reaches the next node quickly.
  • Compare conduction velocity and ion exchange (energy cost) in myelinated and unmyelinated axons.
  • Predict the effect of demyelination on conduction.
How it connects

Built on Part 2

Each node behaves like a patch of the unmyelinated membrane in Part 2: the same Na⁺/K⁺ channel cycle, refractory periods and ion flow. What changes is the geometry: active membrane only at the nodes, with fast passive spread along the internodes.

Planned clinical connections. Multiple sclerosis (CNS demyelination) and Guillain–Barré syndrome (PNS demyelination): slowed or blocked conduction when current leaks out before reaching the next node.
Planned simulation storyboard

What the animation will show, step by step

  1. Anatomy view: an axon with myelin sheaths, labelled internodes and nodes of Ranvier with clustered Na⁺ channels.
  2. Action potential at node 1: Na⁺ influx and K⁺ efflux, using the same channel and ion visuals as Part 2.
  3. Internodal current: positive charge spreads rapidly under the myelin with little leakage (arrows stay strong between nodes).
  4. Node 2 reaches threshold and regenerates the action potential; node 1 becomes refractory.
  5. Space–time comparison: a voltage-vs-position snapshot shows separated peaks at the nodes rather than a smooth wave.
  6. Race mode: myelinated and unmyelinated axons of the same diameter side by side, with distance-vs-time read-outs.
  7. Demyelination mode: remove myelin from one internode and watch current leak out, so conduction slows or fails.

Planned controls

Shared playback bar; internode length; axon diameter; myelin on/off per internode; a probe at a node or internode with the same voltage and conductance trace used in Part 2.
