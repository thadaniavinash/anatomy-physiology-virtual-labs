Key points
  1. Depolarization: at threshold (−55 mV) voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open and Na⁺ rushes in, so the inside becomes positive.
  2. Repolarization: at the peak (+30 mV) Na⁺ channels are inactivated (temporarily blocked) and K⁺ channels open, so K⁺ flows out.
  3. Hyperpolarization: K⁺ channels close slowly, so the membrane briefly becomes more negative than at rest (about −85 mV).
  4. Return to resting potential: K⁺ channels close and the membrane returns to −75 mV. Na⁺ channels have recovered and can open again.

How the signal travels. Na⁺ that enters spreads inside the axon, mostly forward to the resting membrane ahead, which it brings to threshold. That patch then opens its own Na⁺ channels. The membrane behind is in its refractory period, so the signal moves in one direction only.

Simplifications. Only voltage-gated channels are shown (leak channels and the Na⁺/K⁺ pump are omitted). Very few ions actually cross in each action potential; the pump slowly restores them and does not cause repolarization. Values are illustrative: conduction at 0.75 m/s is typical of thin unmyelinated C fibres.

Clinical connection. Local anaesthetics such as lidocaine block voltage-gated Na⁺ channels. Without phase 1 the action potential cannot be regenerated, so pain signals do not reach the brain.
